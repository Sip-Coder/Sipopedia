# Beyond the Glass Winery-Tour Gap Audit

## Anchor

- Video: [Ai Winecast — J. Lohr Vineyards & Wines](https://youtu.be/zm2ECFtViXA)
- Supporting winery account: [A Winery Tour with Kristen Barnhisel](https://www.jlohr.com/latest-news/a-winery-tour-with-kristen-barnhisel)
- Route reviewed: `/#app/beyond-the-glass`

The video transcript contains automated-caption spelling errors, so names, regions, equipment, measurements, and production claims are verified against primary winery, university, OIV, or AWRI sources before appearing in the journey.

## What the Existing Journey Already Covered

- Water entering vineyard soil and roots
- Vine anatomy, growth cycle, and fruit development
- Harvest timing and fruit care
- Sorting, destemming, crushing, pressing, and skin-contact choices
- Alcoholic fermentation and cap management
- Laboratory measurements and sensory judgment
- A generic aging stage
- Bottling, market context, service, and a hosted final sip

## Gaps Confirmed by the Video

### 1. Regional contrast before production

The visit teaches through place: cool, coastal Monterey County vineyards and warmer Paso Robles vineyards lead to different grapes, structures, and cellar programs.

**Added:** `Two Regions, Two Programs`

### 2. A real analytical winery laboratory

The existing route showed an abstract deconstructed glass, but not the working lab described in the tour. Students need to see samples move between production and instruments.

**Added to the main path:** a working quality lab with pH, titration, density, color or phenolic analysis, automated sample work, and sensory comparison.

**Preserved as an optional deep dive:** the deconstructed `Inside the Glass` chemistry lab.

### 3. An immersive barrel room

The existing aging stop used wine-fault artwork, which made the barrel room effectively invisible.

**Corrected:** `Enter the Barrel Room` now uses the complete barrel-cellar artwork and teaches vessel choice, cooperage, cellar conditions, lees, oxygen, and component comparison.

### 4. Barrel-room tools and recurring cellar work

The tour’s barrel sampling is a working process, not a static room. Students need to recognize the tools and the reason for each action.

**Added:** `The Barrel Workbench`

Tools and actions shown:

- wine thief and sample glass
- bung and bung mallet
- topping vessel and wand
- lees-stirring baton
- transfer pump, sanitary hoses, and racking wand
- flashlight and inspection mirror
- sample bottles and cellar log
- rotating barrel washer

### 5. Sustainability as an operating system

The video connects solar power, water recovery, compost, vineyard soil, and operational choices. The previous journey mentioned responsible water use but did not show the winery-wide loop.

**Added:** `The Winery Gives Back`

### 6. A structured tasting-room flight

The real visit includes a guided portfolio tasting that lets students compare sites and styles. The previous route jumped from bottling to the market and restaurant.

**Added:** `The Tasting Room Flight`

The scene includes five modest pours, water, a spittoon, neutral crackers, sommelier note cards, and a sequence for comparing appearance, aroma, palate, finish, quality, place, and production choices.

## Important Tour Ideas Kept Inside Existing Stops

- White- and red-wine program differences: `Two Regions, Two Programs` and `Inside the Crush House`
- Winemaker, viticulturist, laboratory, cellar, sustainability, and hospitality roles: guide narration and field notes across the route
- Vineyard regeneration, replanting, rootstock, clones, and training: vine-anatomy sequence and viticulture visual lab
- Barrel-component and blending comparisons: `Enter the Barrel Room` and `The Barrel Workbench`
- Hosted meals and the human transmission of wine knowledge: `The Table Handoff` and `The First Sip`

## Deliberately Not Added as Separate Stops

- Airport, hotel, and road logistics
- Specific real people depicted as endorsers
- Brand-specific portfolio sales claims
- Monterey sightseeing after the educational visit

Those moments belong to the source trip, but they do not improve the reusable rain-to-first-sip learning arc enough to justify more route length.
