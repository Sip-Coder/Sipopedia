# Beyond The Glass — Story Bible

## World

Beyond The Glass is the cinematic wing of the Living Archive: a connected world where watersheds, farms, gardens, cellars, cafés, laboratories, service rooms, communities, and digital records exist in one visible ecosystem.

The first chapter, **The Journey of a Drop**, follows water as the foundation of beverage systems. The Drop is a storytelling lens rather than a claim that one unchanged molecule literally survives every transformation. The chapter traces connected water systems—from atmosphere and watershed through ingredients, production, service, access, and knowledge stewardship.

## Core promise

Every sip contains a system. Every system contains a story.

The experience should move the learner from:

**Wonder → Curiosity → Discovery → Complexity → Human responsibility → Reconnection → Action**

## Themes

1. **Connection creates meaning.** A fact becomes useful when its relationships are visible.
2. **People belong inside the system.** Beverage education must include labor, culture, service, affordability, and access.
3. **Evidence strengthens wonder.** Sources and verification deepen the story rather than interrupting it.
4. **Learning is a relay.** Planning, sensing, and reasoning work together.
5. **The archive is alive.** Sipopedia is not a finished shelf; it is a maintained network of questions, sources, and shared knowledge.

## Character profiles

### Sippy — Archivist and Route Guide

- Existing canon: Lead Beverage Educator; practical student coach.
- Story lens: **Plan**.
- Wants: to make complex beverage systems understandable, usable, and easy to return to.
- Fears: that fragmented knowledge becomes inaccessible or forgotten.
- Voice: warm, concise, curious, precise, never condescending.
- Visual motif: brass archive compass, espresso-brown tailoring, a small water-blue beacon.
- Story ownership: Origin, People, Access, all scene transitions, and the final invitation.

### Roma — Sensory Cartographer

- Existing canon: Senior Sensory Educator; aroma and palate specialist.
- Story lens: **Recall**.
- Wants: learners to notice how place, ingredients, ritual, and service become lived sensory memory.
- Fears: experiences being reduced to labels without people, place, or care.
- Voice: observant, human, vivid, grounded in touch, aroma, sound, and memory.
- Visual motif: terracotta field notebook, ripple lines, botanical and aroma tracings.
- Story ownership: Ingredients, Culture, Service; contributes lived experience to People and Access.

### Hummin — Systems and Evidence Analyst

- Existing canon: Enologist, Cellar Master, and technical study partner.
- Story lens: **Reason**.
- Wants: every pathway, claim, and consequence to be understandable and verifiable.
- Fears: confident answers built on missing context or disconnected evidence.
- Voice: friendly, exact, gently witty, transparent about uncertainty.
- Visual motif: cyan sensor light, brass measurement tools, source trails, protective rings.
- Story ownership: Process, Science, Technology, Responsibility.

### The Drop — Traveler and central subject

- Beginning: small, beautiful, and apparently simple.
- Transformation: becomes a visible network of origin, chemistry, process, people, culture, access, service, technology, and responsibility.
- Ending: becomes part of a Living Knowledge Card rather than returning to isolation.
- Voice: silent. Motion, reflection, and ripple behavior carry its emotion.

### The Noise — Central tension

The Noise is not a villain or creature. It appears as:

- broken pathways;
- incomplete labels;
- unsupported claims;
- missing source lines;
- distorted maps;
- disappearing names;
- systems drawn without the people who maintain them.

The resolution is reconnection, not destruction.

## Narrative rules

1. Sippy remains the primary narrator.
2. Roma and Hummin speak only when their lens changes what the learner can understand.
3. No scene may make a factual claim without a source note in the transcript.
4. Every knowledge layer must answer: what does this add, what does it affect, and what should the learner question?
5. Technical language must be introduced through a visible cause-and-effect relationship.
6. The learner can understand the complete story without audio.
7. Scrolling backward reverses the same story; it never produces a contradictory state.
8. The Drop never becomes a mascot with dialogue or a face.
9. The Noise never becomes frightening or punitive.
10. The final action is exploration, not a sales interruption.

## Visual rules

- Art direction: editorial naturalism, tactile museum dioramas, and a tech-nouveau archive.
- Palette: Sipopedia forest green, espresso, cream, muted teal, terracotta, brass, and water-blue.
- Materials: stone, water, weathered paper, enamel, brass, glass, ceramic, wood, botanical fibers.
- Interface text remains live HTML. Generated images contain no text, labels, logos, or watermarks.
- Characters use the established Sipopedia silhouettes and roles.
- Sippy and Roma remain clearly adult and professionally styled, but wardrobe is scene-authored rather than uniform: tailoring, fieldwear, hospitality attire, practical layers, and setting-specific color can evolve beyond black suits while preserving each character's identity.
- The Drop remains the brightest object.
- Layer objects use strong, recognizable silhouettes and enough separation for narrow screens.
- The stage always retains one meaningful focal object; no scroll interval may become an empty transition.
- Mobile compositions preserve the subject within the center 60% safe area.
- Reduced-motion mode becomes a complete illustrated field guide, not a disabled animation.

## Educational layer ownership

| Layer | Guide | Visible object | Central question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Sippy | Watershed and spring topography | Where did this water begin, and what shaped its path? |
| Ingredients | Roma | Mineral and botanical vessel | What entered the beverage with the water? |
| Process | Hummin | Filter, pipe, and fermentation vessel | How did people and machines transform it? |
| Science | Hummin | Molecular and sensory prism | Which physical, chemical, and biological forces matter? |
| Culture | Roma | Ritual cup and memory ribbon | How did language, ritual, and identity shape its meaning? |
| People | Sippy | Connected hands and working tools | Whose knowledge and labor made the final glass possible? |
| Access | Sippy | Tap, bridge, and open pathway | Who can receive, afford, understand, and benefit from it? |
| Service | Roma | Tray, glass, and temperature tool | How does care at the final handoff change the experience? |
| Technology | Hummin | Sensor and data instrument | Where can tools improve visibility without replacing judgment? |
| Responsibility | Hummin | Protective verification ring | What must be checked, protected, disclosed, or improved? |
