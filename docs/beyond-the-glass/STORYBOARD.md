# Beyond The Glass — Storyboard

## Chapter one: The Journey of a Drop

The interactive chapter uses one tall narrative section and one sticky stage. All motion is derived from normalized scroll progress, making the sequence reversible.

| Scene | Scroll range | Camera and environment | Character movement | Learning purpose | Transition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Living Landscape | 0–12% | Wide aerial knowledge ecosystem. Clouds, watershed, gardens, farms, production rooms, cafés, laboratories, access infrastructure, and the Living Archive share one connected terrain. | Sippy appears as a small archive beacon near the edge of the landscape. Roma and Hummin are distant moving signals. | Establish wonder and the idea that a beverage begins inside a larger system. | A water-blue signal travels through rivers, roots, pipes, and archive lines. |
| 2. The Signal | 12–25% | The camera tracks the signal across terrain and settles on one bright Drop. Labels remain environmental and minimal. | Roma reaches the signal first and senses multiple traces. Hummin scans it and exposes missing records. Sippy joins and chooses the Drop as the route. | Turn wonder into a precise question. | The focal point locks; near and far landscape layers begin separating. |
| 3. The Aerial Dive | 25–42% | Crossfade through high, mid, and macro landscape layers. The route passes atmosphere, watershed, soil, root, ingredient, production, and service without implying one unchanged molecule. | The trio travels along the Drop’s route. Sippy keeps the path legible, Roma follows sensory ripples, and Hummin traces system lines. | Show that “looking closer” means following relationships, not merely magnifying an object. | Environmental detail collapses into a dark archive laboratory. |
| 4. Isolation | 42–52% | The Drop floats alone against charcoal-green darkness. The Noise breaks source lines, removes names, and shows unsupported fragments. | The guides separate into three partial viewpoints. Each can explain only part of the Drop. | Challenge the assumption that an isolated object explains itself. | Sippy invites the team to reconnect the missing pieces; the Drop opens. |
| 5. Deconstruction | 52–75% | Ten illustrated objects emerge into a deliberate orbit. The active object moves forward while the remaining system stays visible. | Sippy leads Origin, People, Access. Roma leads Ingredients, Culture, Service. Hummin leads Process, Science, Technology, Responsibility. | Give every layer a visual cause, concise explanation, source, and student question. | Relationship lines begin responding across the orbit. |
| 6. The System in Motion | 75–86% | The orbit becomes a connected cause-and-effect map: water affects agriculture; agriculture affects ingredients; ingredients and process affect flavor; service and access affect who benefits. | Roma activates sensory consequences. Hummin verifies mechanisms and risks. Sippy connects the people affected by each choice. | Demonstrate that knowledge gains value through connection. | All visible pathways flow toward the center. |
| 7. Reassembly | 86–94% | The objects return and form a Living Knowledge Card containing a source trail, relationship map, recall question, and linked Sipopedia destinations. | Hummin seals verified pathways, Roma restores sensory memory, and Sippy frames the artifact as something the learner can use and share. | Turn understanding into a reusable learning object. | The dark laboratory opens into the Living Archive lobby. |
| 8. The Invitation | 94–100% | The completed card enters a shelf of future cinematic chapters. The stage becomes calm and fully readable. | Sippy invites exploration. Roma previews the next sensory story. Hummin identifies the next unanswered system question. | Give the learner agency and a clear next destination. | Primary CTA enters Sipopedia; secondary CTA returns to the chapter lobby. |

## Stage composition

1. Background landscape layer
2. Mid-altitude landscape layer
3. Atmosphere and route overlays
4. Character layer
5. Central Drop layer
6. Knowledge-object orbit
7. Relationship lines and active-layer highlight
8. Narration/caption panel
9. Progress and audio controls

## Character beats

- **Sippy:** notices the whole, selects the route, names missing context, connects people, and opens the archive.
- **Roma:** first senses the signal, carries sensory memory, makes culture and service tangible, and restores lived meaning.
- **Hummin:** verifies the signal, reveals infrastructure and chemistry, exposes uncertainty, and protects the final source trail.

## Learner interaction

- Scrolling is always sufficient to complete the story.
- A “Begin narrated journey” control is optional and never autoplays sound.
- Each active layer exposes one “Consider this” question.
- Keyboard users can move through scenes with previous/next controls.
- “Explore this layer” pauses the automatic active-layer sequence without locking page scroll.
- Captions remain visible whenever narration is active.
- The complete transcript and sources remain available outside the sticky stage.

## Responsive behavior

- Desktop uses split copy/object composition.
- Tablet keeps the Drop and active object centered while copy occupies a lower-third panel.
- Narrow mobile stacks copy above the object inside protected safe zones.
- Headline size is clamped to prevent the clipping seen in the reference experience.
- No control receives focus automatically.
- Touch scrolling remains native; the page never locks orientation or scroll.
