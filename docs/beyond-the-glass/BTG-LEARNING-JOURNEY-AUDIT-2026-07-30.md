# Beyond the Glass Learning-Journey Audit

Date: July 30, 2026
Route: `/#app/beyond-the-glass`
Curriculum lens: Society of Wine Educators CSW topic structure, supported by OIV, AWRI, university-extension, producer-tour, and public-health references.

## Outcome

The existing journey already taught the physical life of one still wine unusually well: vineyard water, vine anatomy, harvest, crush, alcoholic fermentation, lab work, barrel work, bottling, tasting, market, service, and the final sip.

Three transitions were educationally missing from the primary story:

1. **Style divergence after alcoholic fermentation.** Learners could leave believing every wine follows the same still-wine route.
2. **Finishing work before bottling.** The route jumped from barrel work to a finished, package-ready wine without showing blending, clarification, stability, filtration, or the final release check.
3. **Label, law, and origin literacy.** The bottle entered tasting and market without teaching students how to read its identity or use the label as a map key.

Those are now primary adventure stops rather than hidden text lessons.

## What remains intentionally outside the fast route

- Detailed France-through-Asia regional study belongs in the existing Regions atlas, entered using the Bottle Passport’s reading framework.
- Full sparkling and fortified production sequences can become future side adventures; the Wine Crossroads currently teaches the defining divergence without derailing the still-wine protagonist.
- Country-specific label law must always be checked against the relevant market and appellation authority. The Bottle Passport teaches method, not one universal label template.
- Medical advice and personalized drinking limits do not belong in a wine adventure. The service stop uses a concise public-health principle: learning wine never requires drinking more, and non-drinking participation is valid.

## Source-verification gates

- OIV definitions anchor sparkling wine, fortification, and international label categories.
- AWRI technical resources anchor stability and filtration language.
- SWE anchors the curriculum breadth and study context.
- Regional, appellation, and national label claims require their own current authority before deeper publication.
- Health and responsible-use statements are framed from current NIAAA guidance and should not be presented as medical advice.
